# Oracles (harness parity B8)

- Status: completed
- Owner: fikrilal
- Risk: low
- Date: 2026-10-12
- Related ADRs / proposals: `_WIP/2026-10-12_harness-parity-proposal.md` §5.3 (row 3.12), §7 batch B; ADR-0010 (credentialed lanes), ADR-0011 (sensors are not proof)

## Objective

Second plan of batch B: a registry that says, for each high-risk behaviour, **where the end-to-end proof
lives** — and refuses to accept a single-feature spec as that proof.

## Behaviour reference (backend-core-kit)

`oracles/high-risk-oracles.ts` + `oracles check`: registry entries (id, acceptance, evidence paths), a
validator that rejects unit-only evidence, and a check wired into the verification profile.

## Design

- `tools/cfkit/oracles.ts` (registry + validator) behind `cfkit oracles check` / `npm run oracles:check`, a
  `full`-profile step (`verify.oracles` in the failure taxonomy).
- Eight oracles: `auth.refresh-rotation`, `auth.account-deletion`, `auth.email-verification-delivery`,
  `http.idempotent-write` (covered); `security.rate-limits`, `users.profile-image-object-lifecycle`,
  `queue.retry-and-delivery`, `contract.parity-and-provenance` (pending, each with the reason its evidence is
  not end-to-end).
- Validation: dotted-lowercase unique ids; acceptance statements; evidence paths that **exist**; `covered`
  requires at least one path under `test/integration/**` or `test/contract/**`; `pending` requires a recorded
  reason. Pending entries are printed on every run, so the gap is visible rather than assumed closed.
- Two new end-to-end suites close the two gaps that were cheap and high-value:
  `test/integration/refresh-rotation.spec.ts` (rotation, reuse detection, session-wide revocation through the
  composed worker) and `test/integration/idempotent-write.spec.ts` (a retryable write executing once,
  replaying with `Idempotency-Replayed: true`, refusing a changed payload, and leaving unkeyed writes alone).

## Constraints

- A `covered` claim must be backed by evidence that exists **now**; the validator reads the working tree, and
  the registry is a claim about the repository, not an aspiration.
- Pending is not a failure state, but it is not silent either: the reason is required and printed, and the
  fixture asserts the registry is never all-pending.
- The two new suites go through the composed worker (real D1, real routing, no test-only wiring) rather than
  through a single feature, which is what makes them eligible.

## Acceptance Criteria

1. The shipped registry validates clean and is never all-pending (fixtures).
2. Covered entries always cite end-to-end evidence; pending entries always carry a reason (fixtures).
3. The validator rejects each malformed shape: bad id, short acceptance, empty evidence, missing file,
   unit-only evidence marked covered, pending without a reason, duplicate ids (fixtures).
4. The report names every pending gap (fixture).
5. A live probe (demoting a covered oracle to unit-only evidence) fails with its marker and reverting restores
   green.
6. The two new integration suites pass, `npm run verify` and `npm run verify:ci` are green, and
   `test.oracles` flips to `present` (26 of 46).

## Decision Log

- 2026-10-12: two states instead of the family's single `covered` state. -> our highest-risk behaviours have
  real but not end-to-end evidence (a 429 that cannot be produced without a policy-override seam, an R2 round
  trip that needs credentials). A registry that forced them to `covered` would be a lie; one that dropped them
  would hide the risk. Pending + a required reason keeps both visible.
- 2026-10-12: the family's list was copied, then re-graded against our evidence. -> three of the eight had no
  eligible evidence at all, so this plan **wrote** two of them (rotation and idempotent writes) rather than
  claiming them.
- 2026-10-12: `http.idempotent-write` is covered by a worker-level suite even though the platform spec already
  proves the mechanism. -> the oracle claim is about wiring (middleware mounted, key stored in D1, replay
  header forwarded), which only the composed worker can demonstrate.
- 2026-10-12: pending reasons name the _mechanism_ missing (policy-override seam, credentialed lane, platform
  driver), not "not done yet". -> the reason is what a future plan needs to act on.

## Completion Notes

The registry landed with four covered and four pending oracles, and the plan added genuine evidence for two of
them instead of grading them generously: refresh rotation and idempotent writes now run through the composed
worker, with real D1 rows asserted after the fact (revoked session, no active tokens; `updatedAt` unchanged by
a replay). The four pending entries state exactly what is missing — a policy-override seam for a 429 through
the worker, credentials for the byte-level R2 round trip, a deterministic platform driver for queue retries,
and a script-level gate for parity/provenance — which is the form a future plan can act on.
